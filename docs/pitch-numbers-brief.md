# Numbers brief — Operational Success pitch (Q&A cheat sheet)

NUMBERS BRIEF — EWR MOVE TEAM OPS APP PITCH
All figures speakable as written. Items marked [ESTIMATE] or [SECONDHAND] should be introduced as "estimated" or "industry-reported" if questioned. Everything else traces to a primary source in the Source List.

=====================================================
1. HEADLINE NUMBERS (the five to lead with)
=====================================================

1. ABOUT ONE HUNDRED DOLLARS A MINUTE. Direct operating cost of an aircraft's time — crew, fuel, maintenance, ownership — is 98 dollars and 41 cents per minute in 2025. Every ten minutes saved on one flight is roughly a thousand dollars. [Source 1]

2. SEVENTY PERCENT. Only about 70 percent of Newark flights ran on time in 2025 — second worst of the thirty busiest airports in the country, per DOT data. [Source 2]

3. THIRTY-THREE BILLION DOLLARS A YEAR. The FAA-commissioned NEXTOR/Berkeley study priced U.S. flight delays at 32.9 billion dollars annually — 8.3 billion hitting airlines directly, 16.7 billion falling on passengers. [Source 3]

4. SIXTY-SEVEN PERCENT. United carried 32.9 million of Newark's roughly 49 million passengers in 2024 — 67 percent of everyone who flew through this airport. United IS Newark. [Source 4]

5. UP TO EIGHTY PERCENT FEWER ERRORS. IATA reports ground handlers who replaced paper with digital tools cut aircraft loading errors by up to 80 percent and cut the resulting delays by 30 percent. That is the category this app is in. [Source 5]

=====================================================
2. SUPPORTING NUMBERS BY THEME
=====================================================

--- DELAYS AND WHAT THEY COST ---
- 98 dollars 41 cents per block minute (2025); just over 101 dollars in 2024. Breakdown: crew 37 dollars, fuel 29 dollars, maintenance 18 dollars, ownership about 10 dollars, other 4 dollars — costs that run whether the plane moves or waits. [Source 1]
- One fifteen-minute delay = roughly fifteen hundred dollars in direct operating cost on one flight, before misconnects and downline effects. [ESTIMATE — simple arithmetic from Source 1]
- 32.9 billion dollars a year total U.S. delay cost; passengers bear half (16.7 billion), airlines 8.3 billion, plus a 4 billion dollar GDP hit. [Source 3]
- FAA's updated estimate: 33 billion dollars in 2019, up from about 24 billion in 2016. [SECONDHAND — original FAA fact sheet no longer posted; cited in peer-reviewed literature, Source 6]
- A passenger's time is officially valued at 47 dollars an hour (FAA/DOT guidance) — a one-hour delay on a 150-seat flight burns about seven thousand dollars of customer time. [Source 7]
- Global airline disruption (IROPS) costs about 60 billion dollars a year — roughly 8 percent of industry revenue. [Source 8]
- Regulatory exposure per disrupted passenger: up to 2,150 dollars cash for an involuntary bump under DOT rules (effective January 2025); up to 600 euros per passenger, fixed by law, for a three-hour delay on flights departing Europe. [Sources 9, 10]

--- GROUND-SIDE CAUSES (WHY THE RAMP MATTERS) ---
- Nationally in 2025, about 30 percent of delayed flights were delayed by air-carrier causes (ground handling, equipment, crew) and another 37 percent by late inbound aircraft — the knock-on those delays create. Extreme weather: only about 4 percent. [Source 2]
- At EWR specifically in 2025: 70 percent on-time; carrier-side causes plus late inbound aircraft account for roughly 4 in 10 of Newark's delays — exactly the slice ground ops can move. [Source 2]
- FAA final order (June 2025) capped Newark at 34 arrivals and 34 departures per hour — 68 total operations — to control delays; 28/28 on construction weekends through end of 2025. [Source 11]
- The fix works: after caps cut Newark from 86 to 68 flights an hour, more than 6 million people flew United on time through Newark in summer 2025 — the best summer in company history. [Source 12]
- EWR handled over 414 thousand aircraft movements in 2024 — more than 200 thousand departures a year crossing the ramp. [Source 4]

--- GSE ASSETS (WHAT WE'RE TRACKING IS EXPENSIVE) ---
- Ground damage to aircraft costs the industry about 5 billion dollars a year today [SECONDHAND, Source 13] and is projected by IATA to reach nearly 10 billion a year by 2035. Belt loaders, cargo loaders, stairs, and jet bridges cause 40 percent of incidents. [Source 14]
- IATA: upgrading 75 percent of that equipment to enhanced GSE would cut expected ground-damage cost per turn by 42 percent — the case for knowing where your equipment is and what shape it's in. [Source 14]
- One basic aircraft pushback tug: about 110 thousand dollars in a public bid opened in 2026. [Source 15]
- One widebody pushback tractor: 300 to 500 thousand dollars; electric versions run 30 to 50 percent more. [SECONDHAND — industry leasing guide, Source 16]
- Gate power and preconditioned air: roughly 100 thousand dollars per gate installed (Memphis), over 200 thousand per PCA unit (Portland), per FAA grant records. [Source 17]

--- DIGITIZATION ROI (WHY AN APP) ---
- IATA 2024: digital ground-handling tools (vs. paper) cut loading errors up to 80 percent and the delays they cause by 30 percent. [Source 5]
- IATA 2026: nearly 38 thousand loading errors were reported industry-wide in 2025; airlines on digital load control report error reductions of more than 90 percent. [Source 18]
- McKinsey: digital enablement cuts controllable delays 10 to 20 percent; controllable delays are about 60 percent of all delays — worth roughly five points of on-time performance. [SECONDHAND, Source 19]
- McKinsey: digital tools in aviation maintenance lift technician productivity 15 to 35 percent. [SECONDHAND, Source 20]

--- SAFETY / LIGHTNING (THE RECOVERY PROBLEM) ---
- FAA-funded NCAR study, Newark data: roughly 25 to 45 lightning ramp closures in one year, up to about 40 hours of stopped ramp work, touching over 500 departures under the most conservative rule. [Source 21]
- Each flight caught in a closure loses tens of minutes at the gate, plus 5 to 15 extra minutes in the taxi queue after reopening. How fast the ramp restarts is purely a ground-ops problem. [Source 21]
- At Chicago O'Hare, one analysis counted about 50 lightning events and 3,000 delay minutes a year, and valued trimming ten minutes off each closure at about 6.2 million dollars annually — at one airport. [SECONDHAND, Source 22]
- OSHA/NOAA joint guidance: shelter outdoor workers and hold for 30 minutes after the last thunder — the clock behind every ramp hold. [Source 23]
- There is no standardized federal ramp-closure rule; common practice is warning at 8 miles, closure at 5 miles — meaning execution and recovery are on the local team. [Source 24]

--- UNITED SCALE (THE AUDIENCE'S OWN NUMBERS) ---
- 59.1 billion dollars in 2025 revenue — highest in United history. [Source 25]
- Over 5,000 peak daily flights; roughly half a million passengers a day on average in 2025. [Source 25]
- About 113 thousand employees worldwide. [Source 26]
- Newark: over 160 destinations — more than any NYC-area airline — and more than 14 thousand United employees in the Newark area. More international departures (over 20 thousand) in summer 2025 than any other United hub. [Source 12]
- United Next: roughly 800 new aircraft expected by end of 2032 — every one turned on a ramp like ours. [SECONDHAND, Source 27]
